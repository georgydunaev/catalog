1. Signatures, Valency (Arity), and Constants as 0-ary Symbols (Sec. 3.1, pp. 73–75)

  • Formulas contain functional and predicate symbols equipped with a non-negative integer valency $k \ge 0$, never semantic functions or classes directly.
  • Since $M^0$ is a 1-element set, a constant is a $0$-ary functional symbol ($k=0$), and $\top, \bot$ are $0$-ary predicate symbols ($k=0$). In SML, constants, predicates, connectives, and operators are represented uniformly by polymorphic Role_Operator parameterized by arg_types : 'Type list and result_type : 'Type.
  • Variables are strictly disjoint from all functional and predicate symbols of the signature.

2. Parameters (Free Variables) vs. Binders & Bound Occurrences (Sec. 3.2, pp. 77–79)

  • A parameter of a formula is a variable having at least one free occurrence (not in the scope of a same-named quantifier/binder).
  • The truth value $[\varphi](\pi)$ under valuation $\pi$ depends solely on the values assigned by $\pi$ to the parameters of $\varphi$: $[\forall \xi\,\varphi](\pi) = \bigwedge_{m \in M} [\varphi](\pi + (\xi \mapsto m))$.
  • A binder of a variable (immediately after $\forall, \exists, \kappa, \lambda, \Pi, \Sigma$) is not an occurrence of that variable; it introduces the scope and binds the bound occurrences within its scope.

3. Three-Argument Substitution $\varphi(t/\xi)$ (“$\varphi$ of $t$ instead of $\xi$”) & Correctness (Sec. 4.2, pp. 131–134)

  • Every substitution and free-for-instantiation operation takes 3 explicit arguments: { with_what = t, instead_of = xi, where_in = phi } (matching Fm.FFI(t, x, phi) in Fact 8).
  • In a bound formula $Q\eta\,\varphi$, substituting $t$ instead of $\xi$ is correct (is_free_for_in) in exactly two cases (p. 134):
    1. Case 1 ($\xi$ is not a parameter of $Q\eta\,\varphi$): Either $\xi$ is not free in $\varphi$ or $\xi = \eta$. Then $[Q\eta\,\varphi](t/\xi) = Q\eta\,\varphi$ (no-op; variables of $t$ are never inserted into the scope of $Q\eta$).
    2. Case 2 ($\xi$ is a parameter of $Q\eta\,\varphi$): $\xi \ne \eta$, $\eta$ does not occur free in $t$, and $\varphi(t/\xi)$ is recursively correct. Then $[Q\eta\,\varphi](t/\xi) = Q\eta\,[\varphi(t/\xi)]$.
  • Semantic Substitution Lemma (Sec. 4.3, Lemma 2, p. 137): When substitution $t$ instead of $\xi$ in $\varphi$ is correct, $[\varphi(t/\xi)](\pi) = [\varphi](\pi + (\xi \mapsto [t](\pi)))$.

4. Bound-Variable Renaming & Wire-and-Box Formula Schemas (Sec. 4.6, Theorem 52, pp. 154–157)

  • Two Failure Modes of Naive Renaming $\forall \xi\,\varphi \leadsto \forall \eta\,\varphi(\eta/\xi)$ (p. 154):
    1. Counterexample A (Inner Binder Capture): $\forall \xi\,\forall \eta\,B(\xi, \eta) \not\simeq \forall \eta\,\forall \eta\,B(\eta, \eta)$ because substituting $\eta$ instead of $\xi$ into $\forall \eta\,B(\xi, \eta)$ is not a correct substitution ($\eta$ is captured by the inner $\forall \eta$).
    2. Counterexample B (Free Parameter Capture): $\forall \xi\,B(\xi, \eta) \not\simeq \forall \eta\,B(\eta, \eta)$ even though substituting $\eta$ instead of $\xi$ into $B(\xi, \eta)$ is correct on the body, because $\eta$ was already a free parameter of $B(\xi, \eta)$ and gets captured by the outer $\forall \eta$!
  • Wire-and-Box Schema (схема формулы, pp. 154–155): Boxing all binders and bound occurrences, wiring each bound occurrence to its governing binder, and erasing the variable names inside the boxes yields the canonical formula schema (implemented by are_similar_formulas in ai_playground/symbol_ontology.ML). By Theorem 52, two formulas are similar ($\alpha$-equivalent) iff they share the exact same wire-and-box schema, in which case they are provably equivalent via fresh intermediate variables.
